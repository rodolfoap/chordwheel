How to use the wheel

The outer ring holds the 12 notes. The inner wheel holds the seven chords of a major key, built on its seven degrees (I to vii). Turn the inner wheel so that I points at a note, and that note becomes the key: every degree now sits over the note its chord is built on. With I on C, ii sits on D (Dm7), V on G (G7), and so on.

The empty slots are notes outside the key. The highlighted notes on the outer ring are the notes of the key; the coloured ones are the notes of the selected chord.

Each degree also starts a mode: the same seven notes, but heard from a different home note. Tap ii with I on C, and the staff shows D Dorian. Use the Tonic and Mode menus to jump straight to, say, A Phrygian.

A little theory

Roman numerals name chords by their position in the key, so the same progression works in every key. Upper case (I, IV, V) are major chords, lower case (ii, iii, vi) are minor, and vii is diminished.

Home, Away and Tension describe what a chord tends to do. Home chords (I, iii, vi) feel at rest. Away chords (ii, IV) move away from home. Tension chords (V, vii) want to resolve back to I.

Seventh chords add a fourth note (the seventh note of the scale counting up from the chord's root) for a richer sound. Switch to Triads for the simpler three-note versions.

The circle of fifths layout puts keys that share the most notes next to each other. One click clockwise adds a sharp; one click counter-clockwise adds a flat.

The numbers under the staff (1 2 ♭3 …) compare the mode with the major scale on the same tonic: ♭3 means the third note is a semitone lower than in major.
